# CAM support knowledge (client journey since 2026-10-26)

## The journey, in order
1. Create video: choose a format on the home page (Clay, Motion design or Attack), give a title and paste the script. Click Review estimate.
2. Estimate: the screen shows the delay of the complete first version (about 1 h 30) and its price in credits. Nothing is produced before Approve & start production.
3. Production: the page shows "Version 1 in production". The client can close the page; the version appears when it is ready. No storyboard, no scene-by-scene step: every version is a complete film.
4. Review: "Version N is ready for your review". Watch the film, read the review notes under it. Then either approve and download, or ask for changes.
5. Changes: write notes (for the whole video, or with a timestamp such as 0:17), click Lock feedback, then Apply feedback. The screen says the notes are being reviewed; the price and delay of the next version appear shortly after. Approving that price starts the next version.
6. Approve this version & download: the approved file is downloadable from the video page and from My videos.

## Credits
- 1 credit = 1 USD on the My credits page: balance and recharge only. Online recharge opens soon; until then recharges are handled by support.
- Each version is charged when it is delivered: the first version at its estimated price, each later version at the price shown before approval. Help support is never charged.

## Rules to answer with
- Never promise a delay shorter than the one shown on the screen.
- A saved note is not yet an applied change: changes are applied in the next version, after the client approves its price.
- The client approves prices and versions, never technical steps. There is no storyboard approval and no per-scene approval in this journey.
- My videos lists every video the client created (Draft, Finished, Downloaded); the video page offers the download after approval.
- If a page shows "We are still working on your video", the version is still in production: no action is needed.
- Do not describe how the film is made, which tools or models are used, or who works on it. Say "the CAM studio" or "the system".
